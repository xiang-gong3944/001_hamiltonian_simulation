# Fourth-order commutator-bound comparison

This benchmark compares error bounds for one fixed fourth-order
Suzuki--Trotter formula. It is deliberately separate from the resource plots
that compare Trotter, multiproduct formulas, and QSVT.

## What is compared

All evaluators receive the same `FourthOrderBoundProblem`. It contains the
resolved Hamiltonian summands, their order, the raw recursive factor list, and
one identically merged ordered sequence

$$
S_4(t)=e^{-itA_K}\cdots e^{-itA_1}.
$$

The sequence comes from

$$
S_4(t)=S_2(z_1t)^2S_2(z_0t)S_2(z_1t)^2,
\qquad
z_1=\frac{1}{4-4^{1/3}},\qquad z_0=1-4z_1.
$$

Consecutive exponentials generated by the same summand are merged by default.
For $\Gamma$ Hamiltonian groups, the merged length is $K=10\Gamma-9$:
$K=11$ for two groups and $K=21$ for three groups. The stored tuple uses the
paper's convention that entry zero is $A_1$; the operator product reads from
the tuple's last entry to its first.

The benchmark exposes three named evaluators:

- `childs_general_commutator_bound`;
- `childs_fourth_order_small_prefactor_bound`;
- `schubert_mendl_small_prefactor_bound`.

Every result includes $C_5$, the time power, individual commutator norms and
prefactors, Hamiltonian indices, product coefficients and ordering, center
$s$, merge state, norm method, and all extra triangle inequalities.

The comparison answers the motivating question as follows:

- Schubert--Mendl and Childs Eq. (M13) are the same coefficient construction
  for the two-term formula, not two bounds separated by a prefactor.
- Childs Table II is likewise reproduced by Schubert--Mendl at $s=10$ for
  three terms. The centered and minimizing Schubert--Mendl rows differ because
  they choose another factorization center.
- The explicit general Childs proof relaxation is not, term by term, a single
  scalar multiple of the small-prefactor coefficient vector. It retains every
  fifth-degree commutator with a uniform worst-case stage multiplicity, while
  the small-prefactor construction has formula- and ordering-dependent
  weights. Its total ratio can therefore vary with the Hamiltonian's actual
  commutator norms and with system size. It is invariant under a common scalar
  rescaling of all Hamiltonian terms because every contribution is
  fifth-degree homogeneous.
- For four or more summands there is no Appendix-M specialized formula to
  compare. A general-Childs versus Schubert--Mendl comparison measures the
  extra proof relaxations and coefficient-vector structure, not a universal
  constant claimed by the papers.

## The general Childs result has no specified constant

Childs et al. published Theorem 6 (Theorem 11 in the enhanced arXiv version)
as

$$
\lVert S_p(t)-e^{-itH}\rVert
=O\!\left(\widetilde\alpha_{\mathrm{comm}}t^{p+1}\right),
$$

where

$$
\widetilde\alpha_{\mathrm{comm}}
=\sum_{\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_{p+1}=1}^{\Gamma}
\left\lVert
[H_{\gamma_{p+1}},\ldots,[H_{\gamma_2},H_{\gamma_1}]\ldots]
\right\rVert.
$$

The paper explicitly says that the theorem does not evaluate the constant.
Consequently, an exact prefactor ratio between that big-$O$ statement and a
small-prefactor theorem is not defined.

For a reproducible numerical curve, this repository instantiates the explicit
anti-Hermitian relaxation in Eqs. (189) and (191) of the enhanced arXiv proof:

$$
C_{p+1}^{\mathrm{general}}
=\frac{2\Upsilon^{p+1}}{(p+1)!}
\widetilde\alpha_{\mathrm{comm}}.
$$

The fourth-order recursion has $\Upsilon=10$ unmerged full stages. This
relaxation replaces all product coefficients by $|a_{v,\gamma}|\le1$, drops
stage ordering, and counts up to $\Upsilon^p$ occurrences of every nested
commutator. CSV and plot labels therefore say **general proof relaxation**, not
an exact constant from Theorem 6/11. Its usually large ratio measures these
relaxations.

## Childs Appendix M and Schubert--Mendl Theorem 1

For

$$
B_j=\sum_{\ell=1}^{j-1}A_\ell,
$$

Schubert and Mendl's Theorem 1 gives, for any $s\in\{1,\ldots,K\}$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
C_5(s)=\frac1{5!}\Bigg(&
\sum_{j=2}^{s}\sum_{\substack{q_j+\cdots+q_s=4\\q_j\ne0}}
{4\choose q_j,\ldots,q_s}
\left\lVert\operatorname{ad}_{A_s}^{q_s}\cdots
\operatorname{ad}_{A_j}^{q_j}B_j\right\rVert\\
&+\sum_{j=s+1}^{K}\sum_{\substack{q_{s+1}+\cdots+q_j=4\\q_j\ne0}}
{4\choose q_{s+1},\ldots,q_j}
\left\lVert\operatorname{ad}_{A_{s+1}}^{q_{s+1}}\cdots
\operatorname{ad}_{A_j}^{q_j}B_j\right\rVert\Bigg).
\end{aligned}
$$

The termwise Appendix-M comparison expands $B_j$ into Hamiltonian summands and
applies the triangle inequality. Innermost commutators are canonicalized up to
sign, so a contribution records five indices from the outermost adjoint to the
base operator.

### Two Hamiltonian summands

For $H=A+B$, the merged formula has $K=11$. Childs Appendix M uses the same
centered factorization as Schubert--Mendl $s=6$. Both evaluators return the
following unrounded coefficients:

| Nested commutator indices | Prefactor |
| --- | ---: |
| $(A,A,A,B,A)$ | 0.004701334310169883 |
| $(A,A,B,B,A)$ | 0.005703818762629350 |
| $(A,B,A,B,A)$ | 0.004638910081589791 |
| $(A,B,B,B,A)$ | 0.007372056645952210 |
| $(B,A,A,B,A)$ | 0.009689668290122170 |
| $(B,A,B,B,A)$ | 0.009726162358456834 |
| $(B,B,A,B,A)$ | 0.017328153057109530 |
| $(B,B,B,B,A)$ | 0.028373434405425900 |

These round to Eq. (M13). The termwise and total ratios are one to floating
point tolerance. A claimed improvement for this comparison signals a mismatch
in coefficients, ordering, merging, normalization, center, or norm treatment.

### Three Hamiltonian summands

Childs Appendix M also contains Proposition M.2 and Table II for three
summands. Schubert and Mendl state that their theorem reproduces Table II with
$s=10$, and the tests verify all 81 canonical coefficients.

This is not the conventional centered choice for $K=21$:

- Appendix M / Schubert--Mendl reproduction: $s=10$;
- conventional center $\lceil K/2\rceil$: $s=11$;
- the norm-dependent minimizing center can be another value.

For the default three-group Heisenberg example at three qubits, the
Pauli-1-norm evaluation minimizes at $s=12$. Thus a difference among these
rows is a center/factorization effect. It is not evidence that Theorem 1 and
Appendix M disagree.

For four or more summands, Childs Appendix M gives no specialized coefficient
table. The evaluator returns `status="unsupported"` instead of silently using
the general Childs relaxation or the Schubert--Mendl result.

## Other sources of differences

The benchmark keeps these choices distinct:

1. **Center $s$.** Every valid center is saved. The conventional center and
   numerical minimum are marked independently.
2. **Expansion of $B_j$.** Primary Appendix-M comparison rows expand $B_j$.
   A diagnostic centered row retains $B_j$ as a linear combination until norm
   evaluation; cancellations can make it smaller.
3. **Merging.** The papers merge compatible consecutive exponentials. Turning
   merging off gives a different representation-dependent theorem bound even
   though the exact product is unchanged. The merge state and full sequence
   are saved.
4. **Ordering.** The resolved repository group order is reused by synthesis and
   every bound. Changing it changes the product formula and may change
   small-prefactor coefficients after norms are substituted.
5. **Norm evaluation.** Scaling runs use the Pauli coefficient 1-norm as a
   rigorous spectral-norm upper bound. Small tests can use exact dense spectral
   norms. Symbolic prefactors remain unchanged when the norm method changes.
6. **General-bound relaxations.** The general proof curve additionally drops
   product coefficients and stage ordering. This is the main origin of its
   large constant relative to the small-prefactor constructions.

## One-step and accumulated bounds

Every supported result has

$$
\lVert S_4(\delta)-e^{-iH\delta}\rVert\le C_5|\delta|^5.
$$

Unitary telescoping over $r$ equal segments gives

$$
\epsilon_{\mathrm{bound}}
\le rC_5|t/r|^5=\frac{C_5|t|^5}{r^4}.
$$

The saved required count is the smallest integer satisfying the target error,

$$
r_{\min}=\max\left(1,
\left\lceil(C_5|t|^5/\epsilon)^{1/4}\right\rceil\right),
$$

with a final integer check to avoid floating-point boundary errors.

## Running the comparison

The checked-in configuration evaluates a two-group transverse-field Ising
decomposition and a three-group Heisenberg decomposition:

```bash
hamiltonian-bound-comparison run --config fourth_order_comparison_config.json
```

Plot an existing CSV without rerunning commutators:

```bash
hamiltonian-bound-comparison plot \
  --data benchmark_outputs/fourth_order_bounds/system_size_fourth_order_bounds.csv \
  --format png --format pdf
```

The command writes separate system-size and target-error CSV files, metadata,
and bound-only figures for the two- and multi-term cases. It does not add these
curves to T-count or CNOT-count figures.

The ratio denominator is Childs Appendix M where it is supported. Otherwise it
is the explicitly labeled Childs general proof relaxation. The denominator is
stored in every row.

## CSV fields

The stable schema is exported as `FOURTH_ORDER_COMPARISON_COLUMNS`. It records:

- model, system size, exact Pauli-group decomposition, group sizes, and order;
- $z_1,z_0$, every ordered exponential, $K$, and merge state;
- bound family, exact theorem/equation label, variant, center, and center roles;
- norm method, triangle inequalities, relaxations, and every contribution;
- $C_5$, time power, requested time and segment count, accumulated bound,
  target error, and required segment count;
- the sum of symbolic commutator prefactors, its ratio before norm
  substitution, the norm-substituted $C_5$ ratio, the named denominator,
  status, and diagnostic message.

The full contribution list makes it possible to audit termwise coefficients
without recomputing the benchmark.

## Computational cost and limitations

For $\Gamma$ Hamiltonian groups, the evaluator needs at most $\Gamma^5$
fifth-degree commutator words. Dynamic programming collapses weak compositions
that yield the same repeated group word, so the cost does not grow
combinatorially with $K$ in the naive way. Sparse Pauli multiplication can
still grow quickly with system size and support.

Scaling runs never form dense Hamiltonian matrices. Exact spectral norms and
exact operator-norm Trotter errors are confined to small validation tests. The
comparison does not currently propagate alternative bounds into T or CNOT
counts; doing so would require holding synthesis allocation and every
per-segment cost assumption fixed, and should be labeled as alternative
resource estimates for the same algorithm.
